Maximum layer thickness

The layer thickness is bounded from below by Params.H_min, through the minimum-thickness entrainment. An upper bound is optional, Params(; max_layer_thickness = ...):

optioncap on $H$needs a bed
NoMaxLayerThickness (default)noneno
TopographicMaxLayerThicknessthe water column, $z_b - z_\text{bed}$yes
AbsoluteMaxLayerThicknessa fixed H_maxno
RelativeMaxLayerThickness$\max(f\,(z_b - z_\text{bed}),\ H_\text{floor})$yes

The topographic and relative caps bind only when a bed elevation z_bed was given to Grid; without one the bed is at $-\infty$ and the cap never binds.

The cap is enforced before the thickness step, as LADDIE v2 does with its Hmax: where the first guess of the stepped $H$ exceeds it, the excess is detrained (model.detr_cap, output as :detr_cap). The detrained water leaves at the layer's own $T$, $S$ and velocity, so the cap thins the layer without changing its tracers or momentum. A clamp after the step remains as a safety net; under the flux tracer form, the volume it removes also leaves at the layer's own tracers.

H_floor of RelativeMaxLayerThickness is for cells where the draft nearly meets the bed. There, the cell-mean water column underestimates how thick a plume can be, and a cap of a few metres leaves the layer almost no room between H_min and the cap, so the cap detrainment and the H_min top-up have to pass all the water that neighbours advect into the cell through a layer of about a metre.

Without a cap the layer can exceed the local water column where its volume budget takes it — as it does in the published Crosson–Dotson run, in 10 % of the shelf cells. That is a known limitation of a one-layer model.

A cap still removes water, and that can cost more than it looks

Until 2026-10 the cap was purely corrective: it clamped H after the step and kept the clipped volume's tracer content. Wherever it bound, T and S then drifted towards the incoming meltwater and entrainment without dilution, which is what pinned pan-Antarctic runs at the T_min and S_max bounds. Capping a Crosson–Dotson run at the water column also collapsed the mean melt rate from 9.9 to 1.1 m yr⁻¹, including in cells where the cap never binds. Both findings predate the detrainment form and have not been re-measured with it.

Python LADDIE v1 has no upper bound at all (its maxD = 3000 m never binds), and LADDIE v2's Antarctic setup uses a fixed Hmax of 10 000 m that never binds either. Prefer the default, and reach for a cap only as a stability measure.

API

Laddie.AbstractMaxLayerThickness — Type

Abstract supertype for the upper bound on the layer thickness H. Pass a concrete instance as Params(; max_layer_thickness = ...): NoMaxLayerThickness (the default), TopographicMaxLayerThickness, AbsoluteMaxLayerThickness, or RelativeMaxLayerThickness.

Laddie.NoMaxLayerThickness — Type
struct NoMaxLayerThickness <: AbstractMaxLayerThickness

Leave the layer thickness unbounded from above (the default): H is set by the volume budget alone, exactly as in Python LADDIE v1, and only the H_min floor applies.

The layer is then free to exceed the local water column where the budget takes it there — as it does in the published Crosson–Dotson run, in 10 % of the shelf cells. That is a known limitation of a one-layer model; see the warning on AbstractMaxLayerThickness before capping it.

Select via Params(; max_layer_thickness = NoMaxLayerThickness()) (the default).

source
Laddie.TopographicMaxLayerThickness — Type
struct TopographicMaxLayerThickness <: AbstractMaxLayerThickness

Cap the layer thickness at the local water-column depth, H <= z_draft - z_bed.

This bound is only meaningful when a bed elevation was given to Grid (z_bed); without one the bed is -Inf, the cap is +Inf and H is unbounded from above. Read the warning on AbstractMaxLayerThickness before selecting it: on a real cavity this cap can suppress melt by an order of magnitude.

Select via Params(; max_layer_thickness = TopographicMaxLayerThickness()).

source
Laddie.AbsoluteMaxLayerThickness — Type
struct AbsoluteMaxLayerThickness{FT} <: AbstractMaxLayerThickness

Cap the layer thickness at a fixed value, H <= H_max, independent of bathymetry: the cap is the same everywhere, whether or not a bed elevation was given. Read the warning on AbstractMaxLayerThickness first.

Select via Params(; max_layer_thickness = AbsoluteMaxLayerThickness(100.0)).

See also NoMaxLayerThickness (the default) and RelativeMaxLayerThickness.

Fields

  • H_max::Any: maximum layer thickness (m, default 100; converted to the model precision by Params)

Laddie.RelativeMaxLayerThickness — Type
struct RelativeMaxLayerThickness{FT} <: AbstractMaxLayerThickness

Cap the layer thickness at a fraction of the local water-column depth, but never below H_floor: H <= max(f_H_max * (z_draft - z_bed), H_floor). The fraction leaves some ambient column beneath the plume. Like TopographicMaxLayerThickness this only bites when a bed elevation z_bed was given to Grid.

H_floor is for cells where the draft nearly meets the bed. A cell's water column is the vertical distance between its mean draft and its mean bed, as if the draft were horizontal; where the two nearly meet, the draft is unlikely to be, and a plume can be thicker than that mean distance. Capped at a few metres, such a cell also leaves the layer almost no room between H_min and the cap, and the cap detrainment and the H_min top-up then have to pass all the water that neighbours advect into it through a layer of about a metre.

Select via Params(; max_layer_thickness = RelativeMaxLayerThickness(0.8)), or RelativeMaxLayerThickness(0.8, 10.0) for a 10 m floor.

Fields

  • f_H_max::Any: fraction of the local water column (default 4/5)

  • H_floor::Any: lowest value of the cap, where the water column is thin (m, default 0, i.e. none)
