Governing equations

This page is the canonical equation reference for Laddie.jl. Equation numbers (1)–(14) follow the description paper

Lambert et al. (2023): Modelling Antarctic ice shelf basal melt patterns using the one-layer Eulerian ocean model LADDIE, The Cryosphere, 17, 3203–3228. The equations are those of LADDIE v1; the changes made for LADDIE v2 are noted where they apply.

For each equation we give the form as published and, where the kernels in src/numerics.jl and src/physics.jl differ algebraically (e.g. expanded out of flux form, or extended), the form actually integrated. The readable per-term reference implementation lives in test/equation_terms.jl, one function per term; the fused time-loop kernels in src/numerics.jl are asserted equal to it by the test suite.

Reduced (dimensionless) density

The paper carries the dimensional density anomaly $\Delta\rho_a = \rho_0\!\left[-\alpha(T_a-T) + \beta(S_a-S)\right]$ (Eq. 7). The code stores the dimensionless reduced density $\delta\rho \equiv \Delta\rho_a/\rho_0 = \beta(S_a-S) - \alpha(T_a-T)$ (field m.drho). The reduced gravity (Eq. 6) is therefore $g_a' = g\,\Delta\rho_a/\rho_0 = g\,\delta\rho$, and the gravity-wave speed used by the CFL monitor is $c = \sqrt{g\,\delta\rho\,H}$. Whenever a paper term has $\Delta\rho_a/\rho_0$ the code has the single field m.drho; whenever the paper has $g_a'$ the code has m.g * m.drho.

The layer thickness is $H$, as in LADDIE v2. Lambert et al. (2023) and the v1 code call it $D$; the equations below are written with $H$.

``A_h`` is the coefficient only under the default viscosity scheme

The momentum equations below write lateral viscosity as $A_h\nabla^2\mathbf{u}$, which is PrescribedLateralViscosity, the default. Under NonlinearLateralViscosity the coefficient becomes the shear-dependent $(C_\mathrm{visc}/100)\,\Delta\,|\delta u|$ instead, and m.A_h retains only its wall-drag role in the grounding-line/land slip terms (it also always sets the entrainment length scale in HollandEntrainment, independent of either choice). Note that dt control (_cfl_number) accounts for advection and gravity waves only — there is no viscous stability constraint, so a large viscosity on a fine grid is not caught automatically.

Prognostic equations (1–5)

The model evolves five depth-integrated 2-D fields. The momentum kernels in src/numerics.jl expand the flux-form left-hand side $\partial(H\,q)/\partial t = q\,\partial H/\partial t + H\,\partial q/\partial t$ and divide the right-hand sides by the face-interpolated thickness, so the variable stepped is $U$ itself, not $HU$. The tracer kernels do the same under AdvectiveTracerForm, and step the content $HT$ under the default FluxTracerForm; see (4).

(1) Layer thickness — continuity

\[\frac{\partial H}{\partial t} + \nabla\!\cdot(H\mathbf{U}) = \dot m + \dot e \tag{1}\]

Kernel _step_thickness_kernel!: $H^{+} = H^{0} + (\mathrm{convH} + \dot m + \dot e - \dot f)\,\Delta t$, where convH $= -\nabla\!\cdot(H\mathbf{U})$ (advection), m.melt $=\dot m$, m.nentr $=\dot e$ and m.frazil $=\dot f$, the frazil-ice formation (zero unless frazil_ice = RelaxedFrazilIce, see Frazil ice). The cap detrainment detr_cap is a sink of the same kind.

(2) $U$-momentum

\[\frac{\partial HU}{\partial t} + \nabla\!\cdot(H\mathbf{U}U) - fHV = -\frac{gH^2}{2\rho_0}\frac{\partial \Delta\rho_a}{\partial x} + g_a'H\,\frac{\partial (z_b - H)}{\partial x} - C_d\,|\mathbf{U}|\,U + \nabla\!\cdot(A_h H\nabla U) \tag{2}\]

Kernel _step_u_momentum_kernel! integrates the expanded form (per unit $\bar H$); term by term, the right-hand side rhs is:

\[\underbrace{-U\,\partial_t \bar H}_{\text{thickness coupling}} \;+\; \underbrace{\mathrm{cU}}_{-\nabla\cdot(H\mathbf{U}U)} \;-\; \underbrace{g\,\overline{H\delta\rho}\,\frac{H_{x-1}-H}{\Delta x}}_{g_a'H\,\partial_x(z_b-H)\ \text{(depth part)}} \;+\; \underbrace{g\,\overline{H\delta\rho\,\partial_x z_b}}_{\text{ice-base slope part}} \;-\; \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2}g\,\bar H^2\,\frac{\partial \delta\rho}{\partial x}}_{-\,gH^2/(2\rho_0)\,\partial_x\Delta\rho_a} \;+\; \underbrace{f\,\overline{H V}}_{\text{Coriolis}} \;-\; \underbrace{C_d\,U|\mathbf{U}|}_{\text{drag}} \;+\; \underbrace{A_h\nabla^2 U}_{\text{diffusion}} \;-\; \underbrace{\mathrm{detr}\cdot U}_{\text{detrainment loss}}\]

then $U^{+} = U^{p} + (\mathrm{rhs}/\bar H)\,\Delta t$. Overbars denote C-grid face interpolation to the $U$-node. The detrainment-momentum-loss term is a Laddie.jl addition (see Entrainment). The front-pressure choice below is described on its own page.

At a one-sided face — the ice front, or a SinkGapsBC gap-sink edge — the neighbour needed for $H_{x-1}$ is not part of the active domain, and masking pins its stored thickness to 0. The depth-gradient term $g\,\overline{H\delta\rho}\,(H_{x-1}-H)/\Delta x$ is then a full one-sided gradient, as if $H$ collapsed to zero across one grid cell. LADDIE v1 and v2 disagree on what to do about this, so AbstractFrontPressure makes it a choice:

  • FullDepthGradient (default) keeps the term, reproducing LADDIE v1, which Laddie.jl is ported from and validated against.
  • TruncatedDepthGradient drops it, as the LADDIE v2 code does at calving-front faces. Measured on a warm ISOMIP+ run, the term is ~150× larger at the ice front than in the interior under the default.
  • ZeroGradientFront drops it and the density-gradient term $\tfrac12 g\,\bar H^2\,(\delta\rho_{x-1}-\delta\rho)/\Delta x$ as well, whose neighbour $\delta\rho$ is likewise masked to 0 there. This is the zero lateral gradient at the calving front stated in the LADDIE v2 description paper; the v2 code keeps the density term.

Neither affects the grounding line, where no momentum equation is solved.

(3) $V$-momentum

\[\frac{\partial HV}{\partial t} + \nabla\!\cdot(H\mathbf{U}V) + fHU = -\frac{gH^2}{2\rho_0}\frac{\partial \Delta\rho_a}{\partial y} + g_a'H\,\frac{\partial (z_b - H)}{\partial y} - C_d\,|\mathbf{U}|\,V + \nabla\!\cdot(A_h H\nabla V) \tag{3}\]

Kernel _step_v_momentum_kernel!, structurally identical to (2) with $x\to y$ and the Coriolis sign flipped ($-f\,\overline{H U}$).

(4) Heat

\[\frac{\partial HT}{\partial t} + \nabla\!\cdot(H\mathbf{U}T) = \dot e\,T_a + \dot m\,T_b - \gamma_T (T - T_b) + \nabla\!\cdot(K_h H\nabla T) \tag{4}\]

Kernel _step_temperature_kernel! (and mat_* variants for array-valued $\gamma_T$/conv2). The implemented rhs adds two Laddie.jl terms beyond the paper: the convective relaxation $-(T^{p}-T_a)\,\mathrm{conv2}$ (zero unless convection_scheme = RelaxToAmbient, see Convective relaxation), and the frazil term $\dot f\,(L/c_p - T)$ (zero unless frazil_ice = RelaxedFrazilIce, see Frazil ice):

\[\mathrm{rhs} = -T\,\partial_t H + \mathrm{cT} + \dot e\,T_a + \dot m\,T_b - \gamma_T(T - T_b) + K_h\nabla^2 T - (T^{p}-T_a)\,\mathrm{conv2} + \dot f\,(L/c_p - T),\]

with cT $=-\nabla\!\cdot(H\mathbf{U}T)$, then $T^{+} = T^{p} + (\mathrm{rhs}/H)\,\Delta t$. That is AdvectiveTracerForm, the form of the paper and of LADDIE v1. Under FluxTracerForm, the default, the $-T\,\partial_t H$ term is left out of rhs and the content is stepped instead, $T^{+} = (H^{p}T^{p} + \mathrm{rhs}\,\Delta t)/H^{+}$.

(5) Salt

\[\frac{\partial HS}{\partial t} + \nabla\!\cdot(H\mathbf{U}S) = \dot e\,S_a + \nabla\!\cdot(K_h H\nabla S) \tag{5}\]

Kernel _step_salinity_kernel!. Identical structure to (4) but with no ice–ocean exchange term (ice is salt-free), plus the same optional conv2 relaxation toward $S_a$. Frazil adds no term: the ice is salt-free, so its salt stays in the layer, and $-S\,\partial_t H$ concentrates it as the layer thins.

Parameterisations

(6) Reduced gravity & (7) equation of state

\[g_a' = \frac{g\,\Delta\rho_a}{\rho_0}, \tag{6} \qquad \Delta\rho_a = \rho_0\!\left[-\alpha(T_a - T) + \beta(S_a - S)\right]. \tag{7}\]

update_density! / _density_kernel! computes the dimensionless $\delta\rho = \beta(S_a-S) - \alpha(T_a-T)$ into m.drho (masked) for the default equation_of_state = LinearEOS. For any AbstractEquationOfState the contrast is

\[\delta\rho = \beta\,(S_a - S) - \bigl(a_0 + a_T\,\bar T + a_Z\,Z\bigr)(T_a - T), \qquad \bar T = \tfrac12(T_a + T),\]

with the scalars from _eos_coefficients: $(\beta, \alpha, 0, 0)$ for LinearEOS, and for RoquetEOS $(b_0, -C_b\Theta_0, C_b, T_h)/\rho_0$. $Z$ is the depth of the layer mid-point, $-z_b + H/2$, in the density and the convective reset, and the ice-base depth $-z_b$ for the interface term of the buoyancy-flux entrainment (14). The default reproduces the linear contrast to the last bit.

(8–10) Three-equation melt parameterisation

\[c_p\,\gamma_T\,(T - T_b) = \dot m\,L + \dot m\,c_i\,(T_b - T_i) \tag{8}\]

\[\gamma_S\,(S - S_b) = \dot m\,S_b \tag{9}\]

\[T_b = \lambda_1 S_b + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 z_b \tag{10}\]

Equation (10) is the linear liquidus. Define the effective latent heat $L_\text{eff} = L - c_i T_i$, where the ice temperature $T_i$ (T_ice_base; the temperature of the ice above the basal boundary layer, not at the base, which is at $T_b$) is supplied per cell by the ice forcing. Eliminating $T_b, S_b$ gives a quadratic in $\dot m$, solved pointwise in _three_eq_melt_kernel! with $\tilde T_f = \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 z_b$:

\[\dot m = \frac{-b + \sqrt{\max(0,\;b^2 - 4c)}}{2},\]

\[b = \frac{c_p}{L_\text{eff}}\gamma_T(\tilde T_f - T) + \gamma_S\!\left[1 + \frac{c_i}{L_\text{eff}}(\tilde T_f + \lambda_1 S)\right], \qquad c = \frac{c_p}{L_\text{eff}}\gamma_T\gamma_S(\tilde T_f - T + \lambda_1 S).\]

The interface temperature is then $T_b = (c_p\gamma_T\,T/L_\text{eff} - \dot m)/(c_p\gamma_T/L_\text{eff} + c_i\dot m/L_\text{eff})$.

The $c_i$ terms are the conduction into the ice of ice_conduction = HollandJenkinsConduction (the default). With ice_conduction set to another AbstractIceConduction, the kernel replaces $c_i$ per cell by $c_{i,\text{eff}} = c_i\,w$ with $w \in \{0, 1\}$ set by the sign of $T - T_f(S, z_b)$ (MeltOnlyConduction: 1 where melting, 0 otherwise; NoConduction, LinearProfileConduction: 0), and adds the fixed flux $q = k_i(T_f - T_s)/(\rho_\text{fw} H_i)$ of LinearProfileConduction to the heat balance (8), which amounts to evaluating the equations above at $T - q/(c_p\gamma_T)$ in place of $T$.

(11–12) Turbulent exchange coefficients

\[\gamma_T = \frac{u_\star}{2.12\,\ln(u_\star H/\nu_0) + 12.5\,\mathrm{Pr}^{2/3} - 8.68} \tag{11}\]

\[\gamma_S = \frac{u_\star}{2.12\,\ln(u_\star H/\nu_0) + 12.5\,\mathrm{Sc}^{2/3} - 8.68} \tag{12}\]

Computed by _compute_turbulent_transfer_coefficients! for melting = TurbulentGamTMelting (the log argument carries a +1e-12 floor for $H\to 0$). For melting = FixedGamTMelting, $\gamma_T$ is a prescribed constant and $\gamma_S = \gamma_T/35$.

(13) Friction velocity

\[u_\star = \sqrt{C_d^{\text{top}}\,(U^2 + V^2 + u_{\text{tide}}^2)} \tag{13}\]

Kernel _ustar_kernel!, with $U,V$ interpolated from the C-grid faces to the T-point: $U \to \tfrac12(U_{i,j}+U_{i,j-1})$, $V \to \tfrac12(V_{i,j}+V_{i-1,j})$.

(14) Entrainment / detrainment

The paper's mechanical-energy balance (Gaspar (1988); Gladish et al. (2012)) is

\[\frac{H}{2}\, g_b'\,\dot m + \frac{H}{2}\, g_a'\,\dot e = \mu\,u_\star^3, \tag{14}\]

(Eq. 13 in Lambert et al. (2026)), where $g_b' = g\,\delta\rho_b$ uses the plume–interface density contrast $\delta\rho_b = \beta(S - S_b) - \alpha(T - T_b)$ (computed in _buoyancy_entrainment_kernel!, not stored). With $\delta\rho^{+} = \max(\mathrm{drho\_floor}, \delta\rho)$ this is solved for $\dot e$ (GasparEntrainment, the default — the LADDIE v1 'Gaspar' and v2 'Gaspar1988' option):

\[\dot e = \frac{2\mu}{g}\,\frac{u_\star^3}{H\,\delta\rho^{+}} \;-\; \frac{\delta\rho_b}{\delta\rho^{+}}\,\dot m,\]

and split into $\mathrm{entr} = \max(\dot e,0)$, $\mathrm{detr} = \min(\mathrm{max\_detrainment}, \max(-\dot e,0))$.

Reading Eq. (14)

The $H/2$ factors are easy to misread as $H^2$ in the typeset PDF. The $2\mu$ prefactor and single power of $H$ above follow from $H/2$, and match both LADDIE v1 and v2 bit-for-bit.

Alternative (entrainment = HollandEntrainment) — shear entrainment after Holland & Jenkins (1999), not a Lambert et al. (2023) equation:

\[\mathrm{entr} = \frac{c_l K_h}{A_h^2}\, \sqrt{\max\!\Big(0,\;|\mathbf{u}|^2 - g_a'\,\frac{K_h}{A_h}\,H\Big)}, \qquad \mathrm{detr} = 0,\]

kernel _holland_entrainment_kernel!.

Laddie.jl extensions (not in the paper)

These terms are zero/identity in the configuration that reproduces the paper, so the LADDIE v1 verification stays valid.

Minimum-thickness correction

update_entrainment! adds a non-negative ent2 so $H$ cannot fall below H_min, and assembles the net entrainment used in Eq. (1):

\[\mathrm{ent2} = \max\!\Big(0,\;\frac{\mathrm{H_min} - H^{\text{past}}}{2\Delta t} - (\mathrm{convH} + \dot m + \mathrm{entr} - \mathrm{detr})\Big), \qquad \dot e = \mathrm{entr} + \mathrm{ent2} - \mathrm{detr}.\]

Convective relaxation

For convection_scheme = RelaxToAmbient, unstable cells ($\delta\rho < 0$) relax T/S toward ambient over convection_time via the conv2 field appearing in (4)–(5):

\[\mathrm{conv2} = \frac{[\delta\rho < 0]\,H}{\mathrm{convection_time}}.\]

ClampDensity (clamp $\delta\rho$ to a positive floor) and ResetToAmbient (reset unstable-cell T/S to ambient) set conv2 = 0 and act directly on the fields instead.

Frazil ice

For frazil_ice = RelaxedFrazilIce, a supercooled layer forms frazil ice that is deposited on the ice base at once. Under the ice cover, the rate is

\[\dot f = \frac{c_p}{L}\,\frac{H\,\max(T_f - T^{p}, 0)}{\tau}, \qquad T_f = l_1 S^{p} + l_2 + l_3 z_b,\]

with $\tau$ = relaxation_time. The frozen water leaves the layer at its own $T$ and velocity: it is subtracted in (1), and the momentum equations carry $-\overline{\dot f}\,U$ next to the cap detrainment, which cancels its share of the thickness-tendency correction. Its latent heat $L\dot f$ enters (4), so $T$ relaxes toward $T_f$ at the rate $1/\tau$. The salt stays in the layer, see (5).
