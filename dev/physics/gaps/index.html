Ice-shelf gaps

Where melting thins a shelf all the way through, an ice-free gap opens inside the domain. There is no ice base there, so no melt — but what happens to the meltwater layer arriving from upstream is genuinely uncertain, and the two defensible answers bracket a large range of coupled ice-sheet response (Jesse et al. (2026)). Laddie.jl offers both, and a physically based variant of the connected gap, as BoundaryConditions(; gaps = ...), selected on the same input mask:

Real gaps are full of sea ice, mélange and bergs. LeakyGapsBC represents them by their cover fraction α: the covered part acts on the layer as the ice shelf does, the open part loses heat to the atmosphere. Running the sink and the connected gap is how the uncertainty of the treatment is quantified; LeakyGapsBC shows where the processes in an enclosed gap put the answer between them. The ice-shelf gaps example runs the sink and the connected gap on the paper's own geometries and compares them with LADDIE v2.

Gap cells are those marked 4 in the mask. Mark them directly, or from a reference ice footprint with MarkGapsPreprocess (see Mask preprocessing). The boundary condition only decides what happens in the cells so marked. Under SinkGapsBC they are demoted to open ocean when the model is built, so the gap edges are one-sided faces like the ice front, with the same open-ocean condition and front pressure gradient.

Connected gaps

A cell marked 4 is ice-free but active under ConnectedGapsBC: the grid's tmask includes it, imask (ice-covered cells) does not, and its draft sits at the sea surface, $z_b = 0$. Three consequences follow, and they are the whole of the physics change:

  • No melt. $\dot m = 0$ by mask, not by the three-equation solve.
  • No ice–ocean heat flux. With $\dot m = 0$ the three equations collapse to $T_b = T$ exactly, so the $\dot m\,T_b - \gamma_T (T - T_b)$ pair in the temperature equation vanishes identically — heat is advected across the gap rather than lost to an ice base that is not there.
  • Entrainment stays on. It is a property of the layer's own shear and stratification, not of the ice above; its detrainment term, which scales with $\dot m$, switches itself off.

The ambient profile in a gap is sampled at the sea surface, so the layer there reads as convectively unstable almost unconditionally; see Convection for how each scheme handles that. Frazil ice does not form in gaps either.

Leaky gaps

LeakyGapsBC keeps the gaps active as ConnectedGapsBC does, with everything in the section above, and covers a fraction α of each gap with mélange and sea ice (default $\alpha = 0.9$). The layer's thickness is never relaxed: it changes only by transport, entrainment, the sea ice the open water forms and convection. Three processes act in gap cells.

Drag. The ice–ocean drag coefficient $C_d$ is scaled by α in gap cells, by the mean cover of the two cells of a velocity face. ConnectedGapsBC applies the full drag in its gaps, as if they were covered; open water ($\alpha = 0$) applies none.

Open-water heat loss. The open fraction loses $(1 - \alpha)\,Q$ to the atmosphere ($Q$ = surface_heat_loss, W m⁻², default 0). After the tracer step it first cools the layer, and once the layer reaches the surface freezing point $T_f(S, 0)$, the rest forms sea ice of salinity $S_i$ (ice_salinity, default 5 psu):

\[\delta T = \frac{(1 - \alpha)\,Q\,\Delta t}{\rho_0\,c_p\,H}, \qquad h = \frac{c_p\,\delta T_f\,H}{L}, \qquad H' = H - h, \qquad S' = \frac{S H - S_i h}{H'},\]

with $\delta T_f$ the part of the cooling below the freezing point, which the latent heat holds the layer at. The frozen water leaves the layer and its salt beyond $S_i$ stays: brine rejection, with the bookkeeping of frazil ice.

Convection. Where the layer is denser than the water beneath it ($\delta\rho < 0$), its $T$ and $S$ relax towards $T_a$ and $S_a$ on the time scale τ (convection_time, default 10⁴ s), as under RelaxToAmbient, and its thickness relaxes towards $H_\mathrm{min}$ at the same rate, so the convected water leaves the layer. This runs in every gap cell, whatever the cover and whatever the model's convection scheme, which leaves gap cells alone (see Convection in gaps).

With $\alpha = 1$ there is no surface exchange and the full drag: this is ConnectedGapsBC, plus convection, and identical to it wherever no gap cell turns unstable. $\alpha = 0$ is open water. It is not the sink: the sink assumes the layer leaves the cavity at the gap, and none of the three processes removes it nearly as fast.

Open water removes the drag, so the current crosses the gap faster and melts more downstream than under the connected gap; the heat loss takes part of that back. The gap layer stays buoyant here, so convection hardly acts. The layer in this gap is 230–245 m thick: it fills the upturned bowl the ice base forms around the gap, and is renewed within about two weeks, faster than a heat loss of a few hundred W m⁻² can cool and salt it to neutral buoyancy.

Within the processes represented, an enclosed gap behaves much like a connected one. At the default cover the leaky gap is within 2.5 % of ConnectedGapsBC on every geometry tested, and even open water stays on the connected side: up to 28 % more melt on the channel, at most 2 % less on the real geometries, against 20–45 % less under the sink. The default $\alpha = 0.9$ describes a gap mostly filled with mélange and sea ice, and $Q = 0$ leaves the heat loss off until a value for the region is chosen; estimates for coastal polynyas reach a few hundred W m⁻² in winter. The sink remains the end-member for a gap through which the layer can leave the cavity.

API

Laddie.AbstractGapsBC — Type

Abstract supertype for the treatment of ice-shelf gaps — ice-free cells inside the shelf domain, marked 4 in the mask. Pass a concrete instance as BoundaryConditions(; gaps = ...): SinkGapsBC (the default, gaps act as sinks), ConnectedGapsBC (gaps stay dynamically connected), or LeakyGapsBC (connected and partly covered by mélange, with an open-water surface that loses heat and lets the layer convect).

source
Laddie.SinkGapsBC — Type
struct SinkGapsBC <: AbstractGapsBC

Treat ice-shelf gaps — ice-free cells inside the ice-shelf domain, marked 4 in the domain mask — as sinks of the meltwater layer (the default, and the behaviour of LADDIE v1.x). Gap cells are demoted to open ocean (0) at model construction, so a meltwater current reaching a gap loses its heat and momentum to the ambient ocean exactly as it would at the calving front, and cannot drive melting further downstream.

Select via BoundaryConditions(; gaps = SinkGapsBC()) (the default).

See also ConnectedGapsBC.

source
Laddie.ConnectedGapsBC — Type
struct ConnectedGapsBC <: AbstractGapsBC

Treat ice-shelf gaps as dynamically connected: the meltwater layer keeps being integrated across ice-free cells inside the ice-shelf domain, so heat and momentum are advected downstream and can drive melting on the far side of the gap. No melting occurs within a gap itself — there is no glacial ice to melt — so no meltwater volume or buoyancy is added there.

Gap cells are those marked 4 in the domain mask. Mark them either directly (a coupled ice-sheet driver knows its own reference geometry) or from a reference ice footprint with MarkGapsPreprocess in the preprocess list of Grid. This boundary condition only decides what happens in the cells so marked.

Motivated by Jesse et al. (2026, https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-2026-4237), who show that the choice between this treatment and SinkGapsBC can alter coupled ice-volume loss by an amount comparable to 1 °C of ocean warming. The two are deliberate end-members; reality lies somewhere between them.

Example

boundary = BoundaryConditions(; gaps = ConnectedGapsBC())
Model(Grid(mask, z_draft, dx, dy); forcing, boundary)   # gaps already marked as 4
grid = Grid(mask, z_draft, dx, dy; preprocess = [MarkGapsPreprocess(refgeo_H)])
Model(grid; forcing, boundary)                          # gaps from a footprint

Laddie.LeakyGapsBC — Type
struct LeakyGapsBC{FT} <: AbstractGapsBC

Treat ice-shelf gaps as connected and partly covered by mélange and sea ice, with cover fraction α ∈ [0, 1] (melange_cover, default 0.9). The covered part acts on the layer as the ice shelf does; the open part exchanges heat with the atmosphere, and the layer convects where it loses its buoyancy.

The gaps stay dynamically active as under ConnectedGapsBC: the layer's thickness is set by transport, entrainment, the sea ice the open water forms and convection, never by a prescribed relaxation. Melting is zero in gaps, as under ConnectedGapsBC.

Drag. The ice-ocean drag Params.C_d is scaled by α in gap cells (by the mean cover of the two cells of a velocity face). ConnectedGapsBC applies the full drag in gaps, as if they were covered; open water (α = 0) applies none.

Surface heat loss. The open fraction loses (1 − α) Q to the atmosphere, for Q = surface_heat_loss (W m⁻², default 0). It first cools the layer, by (1 − α) Q dt/(ρ₀ c_p H). Once the layer reaches the surface freezing point, the rest forms sea ice of salinity ice_salinity (default 5 psu): the frozen water leaves the layer, its salt beyond ice_salinity stays (brine rejection).

Convection. Where the layer is denser than the water beneath it (δρ < 0), its T and S relax towards that water's on convection_time (s, default 10⁴), as under RelaxToAmbient, and its thickness relaxes towards H_min at the same rate: the convected water leaves the layer. This acts in every gap cell, whatever the cover and whatever the model's convection_scheme, which leaves gap cells alone.

The two ends:

  • α = 1: full drag, no surface exchange. This is ConnectedGapsBC, plus convection in gaps: bit-identical to it wherever no gap cell turns unstable.
  • α = 0: open water, no drag, the full heat loss Q. Whether this comes close to SinkGapsBC depends on Q: the sink is not built in.

Neither α nor Q is constrained by observations in this setting; Q can be compared with estimates of the heat loss of coastal polynyas (a few hundred W m⁻² in winter).

Example

boundary = BoundaryConditions(; gaps = LeakyGapsBC())       # α = 0.9, Q = 0
boundary = BoundaryConditions(; gaps = LeakyGapsBC(; melange_cover = 0.3, surface_heat_loss = 300))

Fields

  • melange_cover::Any: mélange and sea-ice cover of the gaps, α ∈ [0, 1]

  • surface_heat_loss::Any: heat loss of open water to the atmosphere (W m⁻², ≥ 0); the gap loses (1 − α) of it

  • ice_salinity::Any: salinity of the sea ice the heat loss forms (psu), which keeps this much of the salt

  • convection_time::Any: time scale of convection in gap cells (s, > 0)
