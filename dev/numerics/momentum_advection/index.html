Momentum advection

Tracer and thickness advection is always upwind. Momentum advection, the momentum carried through the faces of a velocity control volume, is a choice, Params(; momentum_advection = ...):

  • CentredMomentumAdvection, the default, evaluates the advected velocity as the average of the two either side of the face, as LADDIE v1 does. It adds no numerical viscosity of its own.
  • UpstreamMomentumAdvection is flux-form donor cell, as LADDIE v2: the momentum rides on the thickness equation's own mass fluxes, averaged onto the staggered faces, and is taken from the upstream side. Its implicit viscosity is of order $|u|\,\Delta/2$ — ≈ 50 m² s⁻¹ at 1 km and 0.1 m s⁻¹, far above a typical $A_h$ — so the layer is less energetic.

v2's two schemes, 'upstream' and 'fesom', differ only in how the face-normal velocity is built from the staggered fields of its mesh, a question that does not arise on a C-grid; UpstreamMomentumAdvection stands for both.

Building the fluxes from the mass fluxes has two consequences. Walls carry no momentum, because they carry no mass, so wall momentum advection has no effect under the upstream scheme; and an inflow face at the ice front carries the cell's own thickness and velocity, matching ZeroGradientInflow. The first one means that the scheme and $A_h$ have to be chosen together; see Wall momentum advection.

API

Laddie.AbstractMomentumAdvection — Type
abstract type AbstractMomentumAdvection

Abstract supertype for the momentum-advection scheme — how the momentum carried through a face of a velocity control volume is evaluated.

To use this within a simulation, pass a concrete instance as Params(; momentum_advection = ...): CentredMomentumAdvection (the default) or UpstreamMomentumAdvection.

Tracer advection is always upwind and is unaffected; for the form of the tracer equations see AbstractTracerForm.

source
Laddie.CentredMomentumAdvection — Type
struct CentredMomentumAdvection <: AbstractMomentumAdvection

Evaluate the advected momentum as the average of the two velocities either side of the face (the default), as LADDIE v1 does: the east face of a U-control volume carries H̄·ū·ū with ū = (U[i] + U[i+1])/2, and likewise for the other faces. Centred differencing adds no numerical viscosity of its own.

Select via Params(; momentum_advection = CentredMomentumAdvection()) (the default).

source
Laddie.UpstreamMomentumAdvection — Type
struct UpstreamMomentumAdvection <: AbstractMomentumAdvection

Flux-form donor-cell (first-order upwind) momentum advection, as in LADDIE v2.

Each face of a velocity control volume carries the mass flux of the thickness equation — the same donor-cell H·u the tracer advection uses, gated by umask/vmask — averaged onto that face from the two adjacent T-cell faces. The momentum it carries is the velocity on the upstream side of the face. Both the thickness and the velocity are therefore taken from the donor side, as v2 does (Hstar_b(ti) vs Hstar_b(tj) in compute_divQUV_upstream / compute_divQUV_fesom, laddie_velocity.f90).

v2's two schemes, 'upstream' and 'fesom', differ only in how the face-normal velocity is built from the mesh's staggered fields — a triangle-vs-vertex degree-of-freedom question that does not arise on a C-grid, where the face velocity is the one used here.

Two consequences follow from building the fluxes this way:

  • Walls carry no momentum, because they carry no mass (umask/vmask are zero there). That is v2's "No flux across grounding line", so AbstractWallAdvection has no effect under this scheme and the wall slip factor acts through the viscosity alone, again as in v2.
  • An inflow face at the ice front carries the cell's own thickness and velocity (zero gradient), matching the thickness equation's ZeroGradientInflow.

First-order upwinding is dissipative: its implicit numerical viscosity is of order |u|·Δ/2, which at 1 km and 0.1 m s⁻¹ is ≈ 50 m² s⁻¹, far above a typical Params.A_h. Expect a less energetic layer than under CentredMomentumAdvection.

Select via Params(; momentum_advection = UpstreamMomentumAdvection()).
