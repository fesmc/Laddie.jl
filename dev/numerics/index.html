Numerics

Spatial discretisation

LADDIE uses an Arakawa C-grid: scalar fields ($H$, $T$, $S$) and all diagnostic quantities live at cell centres, while $U$ sits on the faces between cells $i$ and $i+1$ and $V$ on the faces between $j$ and $j+1$. Arrays are stored [x, y], and x and y are grid axes, not compass directions. Staggered interpolation operators move quantities between the two locations as needed by the pressure-gradient, Coriolis and drag terms. Boundary conditions are encoded in floating-point mask arrays (tmask, umask, vmask, and the wall indicators) rather than in branching logic, so the same kernels execute unchanged on CPU and GPU.

Advective fluxes of volume, heat and salt are computed with a flux-form upwind scheme; viscous and diffusive fluxes use a five-point Laplacian weighted by the layer thickness. All operators are nearest-neighbour stencils written as fused KernelAbstractions kernels, one pass per term, with the masks applied inside. How momentum is advected, and in which form the tracer equations are stepped, are choices of their own. The upwind switches, floors and caps can be smoothed for gradient-based work; see Kinks.

Time integration

The prognostic variables advance with a leapfrog scheme and a Robert–Asselin filter, at a constant step or one set by a CFL controller; see Time stepping.

Domain, boundaries, and masks

The domain is padded with a one-cell border of land on all sides. This lets all stencil operators use periodic wrap throughout — there are no special edge cases — while the border cells are masked out and contribute nothing to the dynamics. The physical boundaries are:

  • Grounding line and land: zero-flux walls, each with its own slip factor; see Wall slip and Wall momentum advection.
  • Ice front: an open boundary; see Open-ocean boundary and Ice-front pressure gradient.
  • Ice-shelf gaps: either sinks, like the ice front, or part of the active domain; see Ice-shelf gaps.

Before any of this, the input mask can be cleaned up by a list of preprocessing steps and cropped to the cavity, and the ice-base slope is derived from the draft.

Stability safeguards

Several limiters guard against numerical blow-up in extreme conditions:

  • A minimum layer thickness $H_\min$ is enforced through extra entrainment: if a cell would thin below the threshold, the entrainment source is increased to restore it (see Entrainment); a final clamp catches what remains.
  • A speed cutoff $v_\text{cut}$ scales both velocity components by one factor wherever the speed exceeds it, preserving the flow direction.
  • Tracer bounds (Params.T_min … S_max) keep $T$ and $S$ in a plausible range; note that they also hide a diverging run from the blow-up check.
  • An optional maximum layer thickness, off by default, enforced the same way through detrainment of layer water at its own $T$, $S$ and velocity; read its warning before enabling it.

run! also checks the prognostic fields for non-finite values at a regular cadence and aborts with an error instead of integrating NaNs.
