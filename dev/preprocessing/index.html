Preprocessing

A run starts from raw geometry and ocean data, and the model only sees what preprocessing leaves it. Real data carries artefacts: one-pixel ocean holes inside a shelf, shelf patches with no route to the open ocean, pinning points of a single cell, ocean profiles with gaps. Most are harmless to a forward run. Some are not, and for a differentiated run they can decide whether the gradient exists at all.

Preprocess the geometry before differentiating a run

A geometry artefact that a forward run absorbs can make the derivative of the same run grow without bound. In a Crosson–Dotson run built from BedMachine, a one-pixel ocean hole inside the shelf drove a relaxation oscillation of the layer in the neighbouring cell (thickness swinging between about 100 m and 4 m, with spikes in entrainment) and made the tangent grow by one e-fold every ~35 steps. Forward mode, reverse mode and ForwardDiff all agreed, so the automatic differentiation was right; the finite difference of the window-mean melt, which averages the oscillation away, stayed smooth. Filling the hole with FillOceanHolesPreprocess removed the blow-up and left the melt unchanged (9.650 against 9.652 m yr⁻¹ at day 20).

Nothing is preprocessed by default: Grid(...; preprocess = [...]) runs only the steps you list. Before computing a gradient, run the recipe below, and check the tangent.

The pipeline

Each stage has its own page; this one says what they are for and in which order to use them.

stagewhat it doeswhere
geometry ingestionthe 4-class mask, the ice-base depth and the bed from BedMachine-style arraysbuild_laddie_mask, ice_base_depth, bed_elevation
mask preprocessingremoves holes and small patches, marks ice-shelf gapsMask preprocessing
domain croppingcuts the arrays to the cavityDomain cropping
ice-base slopederives the slope from the draft, mask-awareIce-base slope
ocean forcingbrings a 3D ocean field onto the cells, and fills its missing valuesregrid_ocean, fill_ocean!, front_ocean_forcing

The first three act on the Grid and run in that order: ingestion, then the preprocess list (on a copy of the mask), then cropping. Everything is size-preserving until the crop.

Geometry ingestion

build_laddie_mask classifies each cell as open ocean (0), land (1), grounded ice (2) or floating shelf (3) from the bed and the ice thickness, and adds the one-cell border ring of land. Ice-free bedrock is land, not ocean, so a rock island inside a shelf is a wall and not an open boundary in the middle of the cavity. Gaps (4) are not derived here; mark them with MarkGapsPreprocess. ice_base_depth and bed_elevation give the draft and the bed in the model's sign convention.

Cleaning the mask

The steps of the preprocess list, each of which can also be called on a mask directly, and what each removes:

stepremovesmatters because
FillOceanHolesPreprocessocean pockets not connected to the open ocean; they become landnoisy topography leaves them fully surrounded by ice, where they create spurious ice-front dynamics and numerical instabilities
FillShelfHolesPreprocessshelf patches not connected to the open oceana shelf with no outlet is a closed cavity the plume cannot leave
FillSmallShelfPatchesPreprocessshelf patches below min_size cellstoo small to carry a circulation
FillSmallGroundedPatchesPreprocessgrounded patches below min_size cellssingle-cell pinning points and rumples
MarkGapsPreprocessnothing: marks ice-free cells of a reference footprint as gapslast, so the gaps are not reclassified

Connectivity-based cleaning treats gaps as part of the shelf, so a shelf beyond a gap is not grounded by mistake.

Cropping

MinRectangleDomainCropping, the default, crops every full-domain field to the smallest rectangle around the cavity, with a margin of 4 cells. Cropping does not change the result, only the cost, and is what makes a realistic domain affordable on a GPU.

Ocean forcing

An ocean model without ice-shelf cavities gives no profile inside the cavity. front_ocean_forcing assigns each cavity cell the water column at the ice front that feeds it. When the ocean grid differs from the model's, regrid_ocean interpolates it onto the cells, leaving NaN where there is no water, and fill_ocean! fills those so that every column is complete: a column extends flat beyond its own water, and one with no water takes the nearest column that has some. A NaN left in the forcing poisons the run, and its gradient with it.

Recommended recipe

mask = build_laddie_mask(bed, thickness)
zb   = ice_base_depth(bed, thickness)

grid = Grid(mask, zb; x, y,
            preprocess = [FillOceanHolesPreprocess(),
                          FillShelfHolesPreprocess(),
                          FillSmallShelfPatchesPreprocess(),
                          FillSmallGroundedPatchesPreprocess()])   # then MarkGapsPreprocess, if you have gaps

The order is the cleaning steps first, so that each sees the mask the previous one left, and MarkGapsPreprocess last, so that the gaps it marks are not reclassified. The thresholds (10 cells by default) are a choice about resolution: ten cells at 500 m are not ten cells at 4 km.

Checking a geometry

Preprocessing is a mask operation, so it cannot tell you whether a remaining feature is harmful. Look for it where it shows: in the derivative.

  • Run the ForwardDiff tangent for a few hundred steps with respect to one smooth parameter, and look at where the largest layer-thickness response max |dH| sits. A response that is spatially concentrated on one or two cells and grows steadily, while the rest of the shelf responds smoothly, is a geometry artefact.
  • Compare with a finite difference of the window mean. A finite difference that stays smooth while the tangent grows is the signature of the oscillation above: the AD is right and the mean hides it.
  • Inspect the mask at that cell. A one-cell hole or a one-cell pinning point is the usual cause.

If the geometry is clean and the gradient is still large over a long window, the cause may be the switches in the model rather than the data; see Kinks.
