Entrainment

Turbulence at the base of the layer mixes warm ambient water upward into it. This supplies the heat for melting and is a positive feedback: more shear and melt mean more entrainment, more heat and more melt. Where the buoyancy supplied by melting outweighs the turbulent production, the net rate turns negative and the layer detrains instead. The scheme is selected with Params(; entrainment = ...).

Buoyancy-flux entrainment (Gaspar 1988)

GasparEntrainment, the default, solves the mechanical-energy balance of the layer (Gaspar (1988); Gladish et al. (2012); Lambert et al. (2023), Eq. 14)

\[\frac{H}{2}\, g_b'\,\dot m + \frac{H}{2}\, g_a'\,\dot e = \mu\,u_\star^3\]

for the net rate $\dot e$:

\[\dot e = \frac{2\mu}{g}\,\frac{u_\star^3}{H\,\delta\rho^{+}} \;-\; \frac{\delta\rho_b}{\delta\rho^{+}}\,\dot m, \qquad \delta\rho^{+} = \max(\mathrm{drho\_floor}, \delta\rho).\]

Here $g_a' = g\,\delta\rho$ is the reduced gravity against the ambient water and $g_b' = g\,\delta\rho_b$ that against the interface water, at $T_b$ and $S_b$. The positive part is entrainment, the negative part detrainment, bounded by Params.max_detrainment (unbounded by default). This is the reference LADDIE v1 'Gaspar' and v2 'Gaspar1988' scheme, verified bit-for-bit against the LADDIE v1 code.

Shear entrainment (Holland & Jenkins 1999)

HollandEntrainment is driven by the shear alone, with no detrainment:

\[\dot e = \frac{c_l K_h}{A_h^2}\, \sqrt{\max\!\Big(0,\;|\mathbf{u}|^2 - g\,\delta\rho\,\frac{K_h}{A_h}\,H\Big)}.\]

$A_h$ and $K_h$ are Params.A_h and Params.K_h under either lateral viscosity scheme.

Minimum thickness

Both schemes are topped up by a non-negative extra entrainment ent2 that keeps the layer at least Params.H_min thick (1 m by default), sized so that the thickness step would land on H_min:

\[\mathrm{ent2} = \max\!\Big(0,\;\frac{H_\text{min} - H^{\text{past}}}{2\Delta t} - (\mathrm{convH} + \dot m + \mathrm{entr} - \mathrm{detr})\Big), \qquad \dot e = \mathrm{entr} + \mathrm{ent2} - \mathrm{detr}.\]

The ambient water it brings in is at $T_a, S_a$, like the rest of the entrainment. A maximum layer thickness, if one is set, is the mirror image: a cap detrainment at the layer's own properties.

API

Laddie.AbstractEntrainment — Type

Abstract supertype for the entrainment parameterisation — the rate at which ambient water is mixed into the layer. Pass a concrete instance as Params(; entrainment = ...): GasparEntrainment (the default) or HollandEntrainment.

source
Laddie.GasparEntrainment — Type
struct GasparEntrainment{FT} <: AbstractEntrainment

Buoyancy-flux-driven entrainment after Gaspar (1988), following Gladish et al. (2012), as in Lambert et al. (2023, Eq. 14) and LADDIE v2 (Lambert et al. 2026, Eq. 13). The layer mechanical-energy balance

\[\frac{H}{2}\, g_b' \dot{m} + \frac{H}{2}\, g_a' \dot{e} = \mu u_\star^3\]

is solved for the net entrainment $\dot{e}$:

\[\dot{e} = \frac{2\mu}{g}\,\frac{u_\star^3}{H\, \delta \rho} - \frac{\delta \rho_b}{\delta \rho}\,\dot{m}.\]

This is the scheme LADDIE v1 calls 'Gaspar' (physics.py) and v2 calls 'Gaspar1988' (laddie_physics.f90, compute_entrainment); it is verified bit-for-bit against the LADDIE v1 code and is the default.

Select via Params(; entrainment = GasparEntrainment(2.5)).

Fields

  • mu::Any: dimensionless efficiency parameter (ISOMIP+ default 2.5)
source
Laddie.HollandEntrainment — Type
struct HollandEntrainment{FT} <: AbstractEntrainment

Shear-driven entrainment following Holland & Jenkins (1999):

\[\dot{e} = c_l \, \frac{K_h}{A_h^2} \sqrt{\max\left(0,\; |\mathbf{u}|^2 - g\,\delta\rho\,\frac{K_h}{A_h}\,H\right)},\]

with $|\mathbf{u}|$ the speed at the T-point, $\delta\rho$ the reduced density contrast and $K_h$, $A_h$ the lateral diffusivity and viscosity of Params. There is no detrainment.

Select via Params(; entrainment = HollandEntrainment(0.01775)).

Fields

  • cl::Any: entrainment coefficient (default 0.01775)
