Adding a scheme

Every exchangeable part of Laddie.jl is a family of types under one abstract supertype, and the families are open: a scheme defined in your own code takes part like a built-in one. Subtype the abstract type, implement the methods of its family (table below) for your type, and pass an instance where the built-in ones go, Params(; melting = MyMelting()) or Model(grid; forcing = MyOcean()).

A constant melt rate, for example:

using Laddie

struct ConstantMelt <: AbstractMelting
    rate::Float64   # m s⁻¹, freshwater equivalent
end

function Laddie.melt!(m, s::ConstantMelt)
    Laddie.ustar!(m)          # u★ also drives the entrainment
    m.melt .= s.rate .* m.imask
    m.Tb .= m.l1 .* m.S.present .+ m.l2 .+ m.l3 .* m.z_draft
    return nothing
end

sim = build_isomip(; params = Params(; melting = ConstantMelt(10 / 31_557_600)))

These methods are public and exported, so a scheme defined outside Laddie.jl adds a method to them (Laddie.melt! above, or after import Laddie: melt!). Read an existing scheme of the family first: the file named in the table holds its methods, with their signatures and the fields of the model they read and write.

Rules for a new scheme

  • Methods called inside kernels (equation of state, conduction, kinks, the tracer and momentum forms, the advection stencils, the slip factors) must be @inline, type-stable, allocation-free and branch-free (ifelse), and read the numbers of the scheme struct through Laddie._val, as the built-in ones do.
  • Float fields of a scheme are converted to the model's precision by Params and BoundaryConditions through its positional constructor; give the struct one that accepts its fields in order (the default constructor does).
  • Scheme-dependent work fields (m.gamT, m.gamS, m.conv2, m.frazil) are full fields for a scheme Laddie.jl does not know, so your scheme can write them.
  • A forcing's fields are forwarded by the model, like m.T_ice_farfield. Their names must differ from those of the model's other fields (T, S, H, …), or the model refuses to build.
  • Differentiability: guard non-differentiable points with Laddie._safe_sqrt and Laddie._safe_div, and route a new max/min through the kink helpers of AbstractKinks, so that the scheme keeps working with ForwardDiff and Reactant.

Extension methods

The methods of the table, grouped by family, and the helpers a new scheme calls.

Laddie.entrainment! — Function
entrainment!(m, ep::HollandEntrainment)

Holland–Jenkins shear entrainment (see HollandEntrainment), an alternative to the buoyancy-flux form of Lambert et al. (2023, Eq. 14).

source
entrainment!(m, ep::GasparEntrainment)

Gaspar (1988) mechanical-energy entrainment (see GasparEntrainment): e = 2μ u⋆³ / (g H δρ) minus a melt detrainment correction (Lambert et al. 2023, Eq. 14; see docs/src/physics/equations.md).

source
Laddie.melt! — Function
melt!(m, ::FixedGamTMelting)

Three-equation ice-ocean melt parameterisation with a fixed heat transfer coefficient γ_T (Jenkins 1991; Lambert et al. 2023, Eqs. 8–10 and 13). Sets m.ustar, m.melt, m.Tb; m.gamT and m.gamS hold the constant exchange velocities from the start.

source
melt!(m, ::PrescribedMelting)

Prescribed melt rate (see PrescribedMelting): sets m.melt from the prescribed field, m.Tb to the local freezing point, and m.gamT to the transfer coefficient that makes the ice–ocean heat flux match the prescribed melt. Also sets m.ustar, which entrainment needs.

source
melt!(m, mp::TurbulentGamTMelting)

Three-equation ice-ocean melt parameterisation with turbulence-dependent transfer coefficients γ_T, γ_S via the log-layer formulation (Holland & Jenkins 1999; Lambert et al. 2023, Eqs. 8–13). Sets m.ustar, m.gamT, m.gamS, m.melt, m.Tb.

source
melt!(m, mp::UStarGamTMelting)

Three-equation melt with transfer coefficients proportional to the friction velocity, γ_T = Γ_T u⋆ and γ_S = γ_T/r, with r = mp.ratio (35 by default). Sets m.ustar, m.gamT, m.gamS, m.melt, m.Tb.

source
melt!(m, mp::TwoEquationMelting)

Two-equation ice–ocean melt parameterisation (see TwoEquationMelting). Sets m.ustar, m.gamT, m.melt, m.Tb (the freezing point at the layer salinity); m.gamS is not used.

source
Laddie.ustar! — Function
ustar!(m)

Friction velocity on T-points, m.ustar = √(C_d_top (Ū² + V̄² + u_tide²)), with the velocities averaged onto the T-point at the closure_level of the integrator. A melting scheme calls it, since the entrainment needs it too.

source
Laddie.convection! — Function
convection!(m, cs::ClampDensityConvection)

Flag convectively unstable cells and clamp δρ to a minimum positive value so the plume remains lighter than ambient.

Applies in gap cells too, as LADDIE v2's floor does over its whole active domain.

source
convection!(m, cs::ResetToAmbientConvection)

Flag convectively unstable cells, then instantly reset their T/S to ambient values so the density remains stable.

Restricted to ice-covered cells (imask): in a gap, ambient is surface water and the reset would rebuild the sink ConnectedGapsBC removes (see Convection).

source
convection!(m, ::RelaxToAmbientConvection)

Flag convectively unstable cells; relaxation is applied implicitly during the tracer time step via conv2.

Restricted to ice-covered cells (imask), as ResetToAmbientConvection.

source
Laddie.convective_relaxation! — Function
convective_relaxation!(m, convection)

Set the relaxation rate m.conv2 = [δρ < 0] H/convection_time on ice-covered cells under RelaxToAmbientConvection; nothing for the other schemes.

source
Laddie.frazil! — Function
frazil!(m)

Frazil-ice formation rate m.frazil (m s⁻¹, freshwater equivalent) under RelaxedFrazilIce, on ice-covered cells only (a gap forms sea ice, not marine ice); nothing under NoFrazilIce. Called before step_entrainment!, whose preventive bounds on H include it.

source

The AbstractIceConduction scheme as three scalars for the melt kernels: the factors on c_i where the thermal forcing T − T_f is positive and where it is not, and the conductivity of a fixed conductive flux.

The thickness H of one cell bounded by the cap scheme (AbstractMaxLayerThickness): H without a cap, else min(H, cap) · tmask, smoothed by kinks over ε_H. Called inside kernels, as the target of the cap detrainment and of the clamp after the thickness step.

Laddie.apply_tracer_bounds! — Function
