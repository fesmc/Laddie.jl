Frazil ice

Where the layer is supercooled, $T < T_f(S, z_b)$, ice crystals grow in the water column. The three-equation melt can already freeze directly at the interface, but in real Ice Shelf Water plumes most of the supercooling is released by frazil instead (Jenkins & Bombosch 1995; Smedsrud & Jenkins 2004). The treatment is selected with Params(; frazil_ice = ...):

  • NoFrazilIce, the default and the behaviour of LADDIE v1 and v2: supercooling is released only by basal freezing in the melt parameterization.
  • RelaxedFrazilIce: the supercooling relaxes toward zero over a time $\tau$ by forming frazil ice, which is deposited on the ice base at once.

Relaxed frazil formation

Under the ice cover, the formation rate is

\[\dot f = \frac{c_p}{L}\,\frac{H\,\max(T_f - T^{p}, 0)}{\tau}, \qquad T_f = \lambda_1 S^{p} + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 z_b,\]

a freshwater-equivalent rate in m s⁻¹ like the melt, available as model.frazil. The water that freezes leaves the layer at its own $T$ and velocity, its salt stays behind, and its latent heat $L \dot f$ warms the rest. So $T$ relaxes toward $T_f$ at the rate $1/\tau$, $S$ rises by brine rejection, and momentum per unit mass is unchanged. The terms in the thickness, momentum and heat equations are given in the equation reference.

$L$ is used, not the $L_\text{eff}$ of the melt: the crystals form at the freezing point inside the water, and cooling the deposited ice to the shelf temperature is a matter for the ice, not the layer. Gap cells form no frazil; the ice they would form is sea ice.

The supercooling is read at the past leapfrog level, like the convective relaxation of RelaxToAmbient, so the step is stable for $\tau > \Delta t$ and does not overshoot $T_f$ for $\tau > 2\Delta t$.

The default $\tau$ = 1 h is an order-of-magnitude choice; the literature has no relaxation time for a depth-integrated plume. Rees Jones & Wells (2018) find that frazil adjusts to its forcing over a few hundred seconds in a 1 m layer, a time proportional to the layer depth, which gives about an hour for a 10 m layer. Smedsrud & Jenkins (2004) find a residual supercooling of ~0.02 °C that frazil growth leaves in place for up to a day. Ocean models often remove the supercooling within one step ($\tau \to \Delta t$).

API

Laddie.AbstractFrazilIce — Type

Abstract supertype for frazil-ice formation: ice crystals that grow in a supercooled layer, i.e. where $T < T_f(S, z_b)$, and are deposited on the ice base. The three-equation melt can already freeze directly at the interface, but in real Ice Shelf Water plumes most of the supercooling is released by frazil instead (Jenkins & Bombosch 1995; Smedsrud & Jenkins 2004). Pass a concrete instance as Params(; frazil_ice = ...): NoFrazilIce (the default) or RelaxedFrazilIce.

Laddie.RelaxedFrazilIce — Type
struct RelaxedFrazilIce{FT} <: AbstractFrazilIce

Relax the supercooling of the layer toward zero over relaxation_time by forming frazil ice, which is deposited on the ice base at once (no suspended crystals, no new prognostic). Under the ice cover (imask), the formation rate is

\[\dot f = \frac{c_p}{L} \, \frac{H \, \max(T_f - T, 0)}{\tau}, \qquad T_f = l_1 S + l_2 + l_3 z_b,\]

a freshwater-equivalent rate in m s⁻¹ like melt (available as model.frazil). The water that freezes leaves the layer at its own T and velocity, its salt stays behind, and its latent heat $L \dot f$ warms the rest. So T relaxes toward $T_f$ at the rate $1/\tau$, S rises by brine rejection, and momentum per unit mass is unchanged. $L$ is used, not the $L - c_i T_i$ of the melt kernel: the crystals form at the freezing point inside the water, and cooling the deposited ice to the shelf temperature is a matter for the ice, not the layer.

The supercooling is read at the past leapfrog level, like the convective relaxation of RelaxToAmbient, so the step is stable for relaxation_time > dt and does not overshoot $T_f$ for relaxation_time > 2dt.

The default $\tau$ = 1 h is an order-of-magnitude choice. The literature has no relaxation time for a depth-integrated plume. Rees Jones & Wells (2018, The Cryosphere 12, 25–38) find that frazil adjusts to its forcing over a few hundred seconds in a 1 m layer, a time proportional to the layer depth, which gives about an hour for a 10 m layer. Smedsrud & Jenkins (2004) find a residual supercooling of ~0.02 °C that frazil growth leaves in place for up to a day. Ocean models often remove the supercooling within one step ($\tau \to \Delta t$).

Select via Params(; frazil_ice = RelaxedFrazilIce(3600.0)).

Fields

  • relaxation_time::Any: relaxation time of the supercooling (s, default 3600)
