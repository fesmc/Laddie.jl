Melting

The melt rate $\dot m$ (m s⁻¹ of freshwater, positive for melting) is computed on ice-covered cells only; ice-free gap cells never melt. Five schemes are available through Params(; melting = ...):

schemeequationstransfer coefficients
FixedGamTMelting (default)three-equation$\gamma_T$ constant, $\gamma_S = \gamma_T/35$
TurbulentGamTMeltingthree-equationlog-layer functions of $u_\star$ (Holland & Jenkins 1999)
UStarGamTMeltingthree-equation$\gamma_T = \Gamma_T u_\star$, $\gamma_S = \gamma_T/r$
TwoEquationMeltingtwo-equation, experimental$\gamma_{TS} = \Gamma_{TS} u_\star$
PrescribedMeltingnone, $\dot m$ givenequivalent $\gamma_T$ diagnosed

Three-equation melt

Three constraints hold simultaneously at the ice–ocean interface (Jenkins (1991)):

\[c_p\,\gamma_T\,(T - T_b) = L_\text{eff}\,\dot{m} \qquad\text{(heat balance)}\]

\[\gamma_S\,(S - S_b) = S_b\,\dot{m} \qquad\text{(salt balance; ice is salt-free)}\]

\[T_b = \lambda_1 S_b + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3\,z_b \qquad\text{(liquidus constraint)}\]

Here $T_b$ and $S_b$ are the boundary temperature and salinity at the ice face, $L_\text{eff} = L - c_i (T_i - T_b)$ is the effective latent heat adjusted for the heat conducted into the ice (see Ice temperature), and the turbulent exchange velocities $\gamma_T, \gamma_S$ scale with the friction velocity

\[u_\star = \sqrt{C_d^{\text{top}}\,(U^2 + V^2 + u_{\text{tide}}^2)}.\]

Eliminating $T_b$ and $S_b$ yields a quadratic in $\dot{m}$, solved pointwise at every grid cell:

\[\dot{m}^2 + b\,\dot{m} + c = 0,\]

with coefficients (writing $\tilde{T}_f = \lambda_2 + \lambda_3\,z_b$ for the depth-dependent freezing temperature at zero salinity, and $L_\text{eff} = L - c_i T_i$):

\[b = \frac{c_p}{L_\text{eff}}\,\gamma_T\,(\tilde{T}_f - T) + \gamma_S\!\left[1 + \frac{c_i}{L_\text{eff}} \bigl(\tilde{T}_f + \lambda_1 S\bigr)\right],\]

\[c = \frac{c_p}{L_\text{eff}}\,\gamma_T\,\gamma_S\, \bigl(\tilde{T}_f - T + \lambda_1 S\bigr).\]

The physical (melting) root is

\[\dot{m} = \frac{-b + \sqrt{b^2 - 4c}}{2}.\]

The three schemes that solve it differ only in $\gamma_T$ and $\gamma_S$:

  • FixedGamTMelting: $\gamma_T$ is a prescribed constant and $\gamma_S = \gamma_T / 35$. The ISOMIP+ default.
  • TurbulentGamTMelting: both are computed from $u_\star$ via a log-layer formulation (Holland & Jenkins 1999; Lambert et al. (2023), Eqs. 11–12).
  • UStarGamTMelting: $\gamma_T = \Gamma_T u_\star$ and $\gamma_S = \gamma_T / r$ with $r$ = 35 by default. This is LADDIE v2's 'uniform' option and what the ISOMIP+ protocol tunes. A ratio other than 35 (e.g. 70) is experimental.

Jenkins et al. (2010) tested these parameterizations against the thinning of the Ronne Ice Shelf measured by radar, and give $C_d^{1/2}\Gamma_T = 1.1\times10^{-3}$ and $C_d^{1/2}\Gamma_S = 3.1\times10^{-5}$ for the three-equation form. Since $\Gamma$ multiplies the $u_\star$ of this model, which uses C_d_top, divide by $\sqrt{C_d^\text{top}}$: that gives $\Gamma_T \approx 3.3\times10^{-2}$ for the default C_d_top = 1.1e-3, close to the UStarGamTMelting default of $3\times10^{-2}$.

Two-equation melt

The same data are fit equally well by the two-equation form (TwoEquationMelting; McPhee 1992), which drops the salt balance and sets the interface at the freezing point of the layer salinity, $T_b = T_f(S, z_b)$:

\[c_p\,\gamma_{TS}\,(T - T_b) = \dot{m}\,L_\text{eff} + q_\text{cond} \;\Rightarrow\; \dot m = \frac{c_p\,\gamma_{TS}\,(T - T_f) - q_\text{cond}}{L - c_i\,(T_i - T_f)},\]

linear in $\dot m$, with $\gamma_{TS} = \Gamma_{TS}\,u_\star$ and $C_d^{1/2}\Gamma_{TS} = 5.9\times10^{-4}$ (default $\Gamma_{TS} = 0.0178$, for the default C_d_top; change it along with C_d_top). The two forms agree when $\gamma_S \to \infty$, and differ under strong thermal forcing, where the interface salinity departs from the layer's. Jenkins et al. expect the three-equation form to be the more general.

Experimental

TwoEquationMelting and a UStarGamTMelting ratio other than 35 are experimental and log a warning when constructed. The two-equation scheme leaves the interface salinity at the layer salinity, so the melt correction of the buoyancy-flux entrainment sees no salinity contrast, and gamS is not used.

Prescribed melt

PrescribedMelting takes the melt rate as given, in m yr⁻¹ (the units of the melt output), as a scalar or as a full-domain matrix that Model crops with the mask. Negative values freeze. The melt still acts on the layer as it would in the three-equation model: the interface is at the local freezing point of the layer salinity, the layer loses the heat that melting requires (reported as an equivalent $\gamma_T$ in m.gamT), and $u_\star$ is computed as usual, since entrainment needs it. This is the way to drive the layer circulation with an observed or modelled melt pattern.

Ice temperature

$T_i$ comes from the ice forcing, not the parameter set: PrescribedIceForcing holds it (as T_ice_base) as a scalar or as a 2D field, so a temperate ice front can sit next to a cold interior. Despite the field name, it is not the temperature at the ice base: the base is at $T_b$, the freezing point, and $T_i$ is the temperature of the ice above the thin thermal boundary layer at the base — the surface temperature for a steady ice shelf, or the temperature 50–100 m above the base from an ice sheet model. Colder ice absorbs more heat per unit melt — $L_\text{eff}$ runs from 3.34e5 J kg⁻¹ at 0 °C to 3.84e5 at −25 °C — so it acts as a direct brake on melting. How that heat enters the balance is the ice-conduction parameterisation.

Laddie.TurbulentGamTMelting — Type
struct TurbulentGamTMelting{FT} <: AbstractMelting

Three-equation melt parameterisation with turbulence-dependent transfer coefficients $\gamma_T$ and $\gamma_S$ via the log-layer formulation (Holland & Jenkins 1999; Lambert et al. 2023, Eqs. 11–12):

\[\begin{aligned} c_p \, \gamma_T \,(T - T_b) &= \dot{m} \, L + \dot{m} \, c_i \, (T_b - T_i) \\ \gamma_S \, (S - S_b) &= \dot{m} \, S_b \\ T_b &= \lambda_1 \, S_b + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 \, z_b \end{aligned}\]

The turbulent heat exchange coefficients are determined by:

\[\begin{aligned} \gamma_T &= \frac{u_\star}{2.12 \, \log \frac{u_\star H}{\nu_0} + 12.5 \, \mathrm{Pr}^{2/3} - 8.68} \\ \gamma_S &= \frac{u_\star}{2.12 \, \log \frac{u_\star H}{\nu_0} + 12.5 \, \mathrm{Sc}^{2/3} - 8.68} \end{aligned}\]

This results in a quadratic equation for the melt rate $\dot{m}$.

The log term is floored at zero, i.e. $u_\star H/\nu_0$ is taken as at least 1. Below that the layer is thinner than its own viscous sublayer, where the log law does not hold, and the unbounded log would drive the denominator through zero and give a negative or huge $\gamma$. The floor never binds at the default parameters: Params.u_tide and Params.H_min keep $u_\star H/\nu_0$ above about 170. With the floor, both denominators stay positive when $12.5 \, \mathrm{Pr}^{2/3} > 8.68$ (and likewise for Sc), which the constructor checks.

Example

Params(; melting = TurbulentGamTMelting(13.8, 2432.0, 1.95e-6))

Fields

  • Pr::Any: Prandtl number (default 13.8); must exceed (8.68/12.5)^1.5 ≈ 0.58

  • Sc::Any: Schmidt number (default 2432.0); same bound as Pr

  • nu0::Any: molecular kinematic viscosity (m² s⁻¹, default 1.95e-6); must be positive

Laddie.UStarGamTMelting — Type
struct UStarGamTMelting{FT} <: AbstractMelting

Three-equation ice–ocean melt parameterisation (the equations are given under TurbulentGamTMelting) with transfer coefficients proportional to the friction velocity (Jenkins et al. 2010):

\[\gamma_T = \Gamma_T \, u_\star, \qquad \gamma_S = \gamma_T / r ,\]

with the ratio $r$ of the heat and salt transfer coefficients 35 by default.

This is LADDIE v2's 'uniform' option, which is also what the ISOMIP+ protocol tunes (Asay-Davis et al. 2016). Jesse et al. (2026) use $\Gamma_T = 3 \times 10^{-2}$.

Jenkins et al. (2010) fit $C_d^{1/2}\Gamma_T = 1.1 \times 10^{-3}$ and $C_d^{1/2}\Gamma_S = 3.1 \times 10^{-5}$ ($r$ = 35) to observations beneath Ronne Ice Shelf, with $C_d$ = 0.0097. Since $\Gamma$ multiplies the $u_\star$ of this model, which uses Params.C_d_top, the equivalent is $\Gamma_T = 1.1 \times 10^{-3}/\sqrt{C_d^\text{top}}$, about $3.3 \times 10^{-2}$ for the default C_d_top = 1.1e-3. The default $\Gamma_T = 3 \times 10^{-2}$ is already close.

A ratio other than 35 is experimental and warns when constructed.

Example

Params(; melting = UStarGamTMelting(3.0e-2))
Params(; melting = UStarGamTMelting(3.0e-2, 70.0))   # Γ_T / Γ_S = 70

Fields

  • Gamma_T::Any: dimensionless heat transfer coefficient (default 3.0e-2)

  • ratio::Any: ratio of the heat to the salt transfer coefficient, $\gamma_T/\gamma_S$ (default 35)

Laddie.TwoEquationMelting — Type
struct TwoEquationMelting{FT} <: AbstractMelting

Two-equation ice–ocean melt parameterisation (McPhee 1992; Jenkins et al. 2010, Eqs. 14–15). The interface is at the freezing point of the layer salinity, $T_b = T_f(S, z_b)$, so the salt balance drops out, and the heat transfer coefficient $\gamma_{TS} = \Gamma_{TS}\,u_\star$ absorbs the effect of salt diffusion:

\[c_p\,\gamma_{TS}\,(T - T_b) = \dot m\,L + \dot m\,c_i\,(T_b - T_i) + q_\text{cond}\]

($q_\text{cond}$ and the $c_i$ term from AbstractIceConduction, the Holland & Jenkins form by default.) Unlike the three-equation form it is linear in $\dot m$,

\[\dot m = \frac{c_p\,\gamma_{TS}\,(T - T_f) - q_\text{cond}}{L - c_{i}\,(T_i - T_f)} .\]

It is as good as the three-equation form at the one site where Jenkins et al. tested both, and expected to be less so under strong thermal forcing, where the interface salinity departs from the layer's: $\Gamma_T > \Gamma_{TS}$ always (Jenkins et al. 2010, Eq. 8). The buoyancy-flux entrainment reads the interface salinity $S_b = S$ of this scheme, so the buoyancy of the meltwater enters only through the thickness, not through the entrainment's melt correction. gamS is not used and stays zero.

Jenkins et al. (2010) fit $C_d^{1/2}\Gamma_{TS} = 5.9 \times 10^{-4}$ ($\Gamma_{TS}$ = 0.006 with $C_d$ = 0.0097; McPhee 1992). Since $\Gamma_{TS}$ multiplies the $u_\star$ of this model, which uses Params.C_d_top, the equivalent is $5.9 \times 10^{-4}/\sqrt{C_d^\text{top}}$, which is the default 0.0178 for C_d_top = 1.1e-3. Change it along with C_d_top.

Select via Params(; melting = TwoEquationMelting()); warns when constructed.

Fields

  • Gamma_TS::Any: dimensionless heat transfer coefficient (default 0.0178)

Laddie.PrescribedMelting — Type
struct PrescribedMelting{M} <: AbstractMelting

Prescribed melt rate, $\dot{m} = \dot{m}_0$, applied on ice-covered cells only (gap cells never melt).

melt is in m yr⁻¹ of freshwater — the same units as the model's melt output — and may be a scalar or a full-domain matrix the same size as the mask passed to Grid; Model crops a matrix alongside the mask. Negative values freeze.

The melt still acts on the layer as in the three-equation model, so a prescribed melt rate cools and freshens the plume consistently:

  • the interface temperature is the local freezing point, $T_b = \lambda_1 S + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 z_b$;
  • the layer loses the heat that melting requires, $\gamma_T (T - T_b) = \dot{m}\,(L - c_i (T_i - T_b)) / c_p$, with the equivalent $\gamma_T$ reported as m.gamT;
  • the friction velocity $u_\star$ is computed as usual, since entrainment needs it.

Example

Params(; melting = PrescribedMelting(10.0))          # uniform 10 m/yr
Params(; melting = PrescribedMelting(melt_matrix))   # 2D, same size as `mask`

Fields

  • melt::Any: prescribed melt rate (m yr⁻¹ freshwater), a scalar or a matrix the size of mask (default 0)
