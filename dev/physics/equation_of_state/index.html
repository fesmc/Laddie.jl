Equation of state

The equation of state sets the buoyancy of the layer against the ambient cavity water, as the reduced density contrast

\[\delta\rho = \frac{\rho_a - \rho}{\rho_0},\]

which pushes the light meltwater up the draft. It is the one place where the EOS enters Laddie.jl: through $\delta\rho$ in the pressure gradient, the entrainment and the convective stability.

Every equation of state has the form

\[\delta\rho = \beta\,(S_a - S) - \bigl(a_0 + a_T\,\bar T + a_Z\,Z\bigr)(T_a - T), \qquad \bar T = \tfrac12 (T_a + T),\]

with $Z$ the depth of the layer mid-point, $-z_b + H/2$. The thermal expansion $\alpha = a_0 + a_T \bar T + a_Z Z$ is linear in temperature and in depth, and the equations of state differ only in the four coefficients. The seawater equation of state is nonlinear mostly through $\alpha$, which falls towards the freezing point (cabbeling) and rises with depth (thermobaricity).

equation of state$\delta\rho$$\alpha$
LinearEOS (default)$\beta\,\Delta S - \alpha\,\Delta T$constant, Params.alpha
RoquetEOS$\beta\,\Delta S - (a_0 + a_T \bar T + a_Z Z)\,\Delta T$$\bigl[C_b(\bar\Theta - \Theta_0) + T_h Z\bigr]/\rho_0$

where $\Delta X = X_a - X$.

Linear

LinearEOS uses the constants Params.alpha and Params.beta ($a_T = a_Z = 0$). It is the equation of state of LADDIE v1 and v2, and reproduces their contrast to the last bit.

Roquet et al. (2015)

RoquetEOS is the simplified polynomial of Roquet et al. (2015) (their Eq. 17). In the paper's own variables, Conservative Temperature $\Theta$ (°C), Absolute Salinity $S_A$ (g kg⁻¹) and depth $Z$ (m), the density anomaly is

\[\rho'(\Theta, S_A, Z) = -\tfrac{C_b}{2}\,(\Theta - \Theta_0)^2 - T_h\,Z\,\Theta + b_0\,S_A,\]

with $C_b = 0.011$ kg m⁻³ K⁻², $\Theta_0 = -4.5$ °C, $T_h = 2.5\times10^{-5}$ kg m⁻⁴ K⁻¹ and $b_0 = 0.77$ kg m⁻³ (g kg⁻¹)⁻¹.

To use it in the form of the equation of state above, take the difference between the ambient and the layer, $\rho_a - \rho = \rho'(\Theta_a, S_{A,a}, Z) - \rho'(\Theta, S_A, Z)$. The quadratic difference factorises, $(\Theta_a-\Theta_0)^2 - (\Theta-\Theta_0)^2 = 2\,(\Theta_a-\Theta)(\bar\Theta-\Theta_0)$ with $\bar\Theta = \tfrac12(\Theta_a+\Theta)$, so

\[\rho_a - \rho = b_0\,(S_{A,a} - S_A) - \bigl[C_b\,(\bar\Theta - \Theta_0) + T_h Z\bigr](\Theta_a - \Theta).\]

Dividing by $\rho_0$ (Params.rho0_seawater) gives the dimensionless form, with $\beta = b_0/\rho_0$, $a_0 = -C_b\Theta_0/\rho_0$, $a_T = C_b/\rho_0$ and $a_Z = T_h/\rho_0$, which is the coefficient set _eos_coefficients uses for RoquetEOS.

The thermal expansion is then $\alpha = \bigl[C_b(\bar\Theta - \Theta_0) + T_h Z\bigr]/\rho_0$, or $0.029$ kg m⁻³ K⁻¹ at the surface freezing point (about $-1.9$ °C), $0.041$ at 500 m, and $0.068$ at 0.5 °C and 500 m, against the constant $0.038$ of the linear default. So the buoyancy contrast of a plume against its ambient is weaker where it is warm or deep (by about 5 % for a cold plume and 25 % for a warm one at 500 m).

Experimental

RoquetEOS is experimental and logs a warning when constructed. Roquet et al. (2015) fit it to the open ocean in Conservative Temperature and Absolute Salinity, which Laddie takes as its temperature and salinity (only salinity differences enter, so the offset to practical salinity cancels), and say it serves understanding rather than realistic simulation (TEOS-10 does). Supercooled cavity water is an extrapolation. Params.alpha and Params.beta are used by LinearEOS only, and an ambient profile built as neutral for them (as in the examples) is not neutral for another equation of state.

API

Laddie.AbstractEquationOfState — Type

Abstract supertype for the equation of state: the buoyancy contrast $\delta\rho = (\rho_a - \rho)/\rho_0$ of the ambient water against the layer, which sets the pressure gradient, the entrainment and the convective stability. Pass a concrete instance as Params(; equation_of_state = ...):

  • LinearEOS (the default)
  • RoquetEOS

All contrasts have the form

\[\delta\rho = \beta\,(S_a - S) - \bigl(a_0 + a_T\,\bar T + a_Z\,Z\bigr)\,(T_a - T), \qquad \bar T = \tfrac12 (T_a + T),\]

with $Z$ the depth of the layer mid-point (positive downward), so that the thermal expansion $\alpha = a_0 + a_T \bar T + a_Z Z$ is linear in temperature (cabbeling) and depth (thermobaricity). The parameterisations differ only in the four coefficients, which are scalars computed where the kernels are launched.

Laddie.RoquetEOS — Type
struct RoquetEOS{FT} <: AbstractEquationOfState

The simplified polynomial equation of state of Roquet et al. (2015, their Eq. 17),

\[\rho' = -\frac{C_b}{2}\,(\Theta - \Theta_0)^2 - T_h\,Z\,\Theta + b_0\,S_A,\]

the simplest form that has both cabbeling (the thermal expansion falls with temperature) and thermobaricity (it rises with depth) and whose parameters are directly interpretable: $C_b$ the sensitivity of $\alpha$ to $\Theta$, $\Theta_0$ the temperature at which the surface $\alpha$ vanishes, $T_h$ its sensitivity to depth, $b_0$ the (constant) haline contraction. The thermal expansion is $\alpha = C_b(\Theta - \Theta_0) + T_h Z$, i.e. 0.029 kg m⁻³ K⁻¹ at the surface freezing point (−1.9 °C), 0.041 at 500 m, and 0.068 at 0.5 °C and 500 m, where the linear default has 0.038 throughout. The defaults are the values of the paper. The constants are rescaled by $\rho_0$ (Params.rho0_seawater) to the reduced contrast of the model.

Params.alpha and Params.beta are not used by this equation of state.

Experimental

Constructing a RoquetEOS logs a warning. Roquet et al. (2015) fit it to the open ocean ($\Theta$ down to −1.9 °C, $S_A$ ≈ 34–35 g kg⁻¹, $Z$ to 5000 m) and in Conservative Temperature and Absolute Salinity, which this model takes as its temperature and salinity. Since $b_0$ is constant, only salinity differences enter and the offset between Absolute and Practical Salinity cancels; the difference between Conservative and potential temperature is below 0.01 K in cold water. Supercooled cavity water is an extrapolation. They stress that it serves understanding rather than realistic simulation, for which they recommend TEOS-10; what it adds here is a thermal expansion that is right near the freezing point, which their simulations show matters most for polar stratification.

Example

Params(; equation_of_state = RoquetEOS())

Fields

  • Cb::Any: sensitivity of the thermal expansion to temperature $C_b$ (kg m⁻³ K⁻², default 0.011); non-negative

  • Theta0::Any: temperature at which the surface thermal expansion vanishes $\Theta_0$ (°C, default -4.5)

  • Th::Any: sensitivity of the thermal expansion to depth $T_h$ (kg m⁻⁴ K⁻¹, default 2.5e-5)

  • b0::Any: haline contraction $b_0$ (kg m⁻³ (g kg⁻¹)⁻¹, default 0.77); positive
