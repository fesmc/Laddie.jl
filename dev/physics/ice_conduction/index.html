Ice conduction

The interface heat budget has an ice-side term, the heat conducted into the ice (Wiskandt & Jourdain (2025), Eqs. 1–2):

\[\dot m\,L = c_p\,\gamma_T\,(T - T_b) + q_\text{cond},\]

with $q_\text{cond}$ negative for an ice surface colder than the base. The ice column is not resolved, so $q_\text{cond}$ is a parameterisation, selected with Params(; ice_conduction = ...).

The default term $-c_i (T_i - T_b)\,\dot m$ in $L_\text{eff}$ (see Melting) is the linearisation of Holland & Jenkins (1999), which assumes that the ice velocity is proportional to the melt rate. The other parameterisations are those compared by Wiskandt & Jourdain (2025):

parameterisationheat conducted into the icewhen it is reasonable
HollandJenkinsConduction (default)$\dot m\,c_i\,(T_b - T_i)$, melting and refreezingmelting; the v1 and v2 behaviour
MeltOnlyConductionthe same for $\dot m > 0$, zero otherwiseas above, and a refreezing base of marine ice near the freezing point
NoConductionnone, $L_\text{eff} = L$marine ice only; overestimates melt by $c_i (T_s - T_f)/L$ (≈ 11 % for $T_s = -20$ °C)
LinearProfileConduction$k_i (T_f - T_s)/H_i$, independent of $\dot m$thick, slow ice shelves in thermal steady state (e.g. Ross)

The switch of MeltOnlyConduction is the sign of $T - T_f(S, z_b)$, which is the sign of $\dot m$, so the melt equations are solved once. LinearProfileConduction takes $T_f$ at the layer salinity so that the flux does not depend on $\dot m$ and the equation stays quadratic, and the ice thickness from the draft by hydrostatic equilibrium, $H_i = -z_b\,\rho_0/\rho_\text{ice}$. A thermal steady state between base and surface takes of order $(H_i/2)^2/\kappa_i$, about 1000 years for 400 m of ice, which only the large, slow ice shelves reach.

In the kernels, every parameterisation reduces to two factors on $c_i$ (where the layer melts and where it does not) and a fixed conductive flux, which amounts to evaluating the melt equations at $T - q/(c_p\gamma_T)$ in place of $T$; see the equation reference.

Experimental

Every parameterisation but the default is experimental, and constructing one logs a warning. The T_ice_base of the ice forcing is used as the ice temperature $T_i$ by all of them. In Holland & Jenkins (1999) and Wiskandt & Jourdain (2025) this is the ice-shelf surface temperature $T_s$ for every parameterisation (the ice at the base itself is at the freezing point); Wiskandt & Jourdain use the 1980–2019 mean snow-surface temperature.

API

Laddie.AbstractIceConduction — Type

Abstract supertype for the heat conducted into the ice at the ice–ocean interface, the ice-side term of the interface heat budget (Wiskandt & Jourdain 2025, Eqs. 1–2):

\[\dot m\,L = c_p\,\gamma_T\,(T - T_b) + q_\text{cond},\]

with $q_\text{cond}$ (per unit mass of water, like the other terms) the conduction into the ice, negative for a surface colder than the base. Pass a concrete instance as Params(; ice_conduction = ...):

  • HollandJenkinsConduction (the default)
  • MeltOnlyConduction
  • NoConduction
  • LinearProfileConduction

All except the default are experimental and warn when constructed. The ice temperature $T_i$ they use is the T_ice_base of the ice forcing (PrescribedIceForcing), which plays the role of the ice surface temperature $T_s$ of the paper.

source
Laddie.HollandJenkinsConduction — Type
struct HollandJenkinsConduction <: AbstractIceConduction

Holland & Jenkins (1999) linearisation of the heat advected by the ice, their melt-rate dependent gradient: $q_\text{cond} = \dot m\,c_i\,(T_b - T_i)$, so the latent heat becomes $L_\text{eff} = L - c_i (T_i - T_b)$. Applied for melting and refreezing alike. The default, and the behaviour of LADDIE v1 and v2.

It is Eq. (2C) of Wiskandt & Jourdain (2025) without the cut-off at $\dot m = 0$, see MeltOnlyConduction.

source
Laddie.MeltOnlyConduction — Type
struct MeltOnlyConduction <: AbstractIceConduction

The parameterisation of HollandJenkinsConduction, but with no conduction into the ice where the layer is not melting it: $q_\text{cond} = \dot m\,c_i\,(T_b - T_i)$ for $\dot m > 0$, zero otherwise (Wiskandt & Jourdain 2025, Eq. 2C with $\max\{\dot m, 0\}$). Their argument is that a refreezing ice base carries marine ice close to the freezing point throughout, so there is no gradient to conduct along.

The switch is the sign of the thermal forcing $T - T_f(S, z_b)$, which has the sign of $\dot m$ and needs no iteration of the melt equations. Where it is not positive, the latent heat is the plain $L$.

Select via Params(; ice_conduction = MeltOnlyConduction()); warns when constructed.

source
Laddie.NoConduction — Type
struct NoConduction <: AbstractIceConduction

No conduction into the ice, $q_\text{cond} = 0$ (Wiskandt & Jourdain 2025, Eq. 2A), so $L_\text{eff} = L$ and T_ice_base has no effect. It is a good approximation only for a base of marine ice at the freezing point. Overestimates the melt by $c_i (T_s - T_f)/L$, about 11 % for $T_s = -20$ °C, compared with MeltOnlyConduction.

Select via Params(; ice_conduction = NoConduction()); warns when constructed.

source
Laddie.LinearProfileConduction — Type
struct LinearProfileConduction{FT} <: AbstractIceConduction

Steady linear temperature profile through the ice, with no advection (Wiskandt & Jourdain 2025, Eq. 2B; used by Losch 2008, Holland et al. 2008):

\[q_\text{cond} = \frac{k_i\,(T_f - T_s)}{\rho_\text{fw}\,H_i},\]

with $T_f$ the freezing point at the layer salinity (so that $q_\text{cond}$ does not depend on $\dot m$ and the melt equation stays quadratic), $T_s$ the T_ice_base of the ice forcing, and $H_i$ the ice thickness, taken from the draft by hydrostatic equilibrium, $H_i = -z_b\,\rho_0/\rho_\text{ice}$ with Params.rho0_seawater and Params.rho_ice. The flux acts for melting and refreezing alike. A thermal steady state between base and surface takes of order $(H_i/2)^2/\kappa_i$ (about 1000 years for 400 m), which only the large, slow ice shelves reach.

Select via Params(; ice_conduction = LinearProfileConduction()); warns when constructed.

Fields

  • conductivity::Any: thermal conductivity of ice $k_i = \rho_i c_i \kappa_i$ (W m⁻¹ K⁻¹, default 2.1; Jenkins et al. 2010)
